About this model

What This Shows

The first pyramid model drew the poloidal core as fixed lines — the semi-spiral, the bubble at the Queen's Chamber, the spectrum of helices at the King's Chamber, and so on up through the apex. This companion piece lets the flow itself do the drawing. Particles spiral in under the base, climb the central column, and leave trails; the familiar shapes appear as the places where those trails gather.

In other words, the static picture is what the moving picture leaves behind. Turn on Long trails in the play bar and give it half a minute — the column fills in until it reads like the first model.

How the Motion Builds the Shapes

A steady flow. The field here doesn't change from moment to moment, so the path any particle follows is a streamline. That is why trails laid down by many particles add up to a stable form.

Speed follows the channel. Where the column narrows the flow speeds up; where it widens, or presses against a stagnation point, it slows. Watch the particles crawl through the pinch at the top of the semi-spiral and race up the cone toward the apex. Slow places crowd with particles and glow brighter — the stillest places look the fullest.

Spin follows radius. Angular momentum is conserved, so swirl grows as the flow pulls inward. At the pinch the particles barely rise but spin hard; that is the tightening corkscrew of the semi-spiral, and it is what tips the flow into breakdown.

The bubble turns over. At the lower stillpoint part of the flow stalls and is caught. Those particles (green) rise up the outside of the cell, turn in at the top, fall down the axis, and circle again a time or two before slipping out the top. Their spin keeps its sense; only the up-and-down circulation reverses. The rest of the flow (gold) parts around the cell.

Order forms and dissolves. In the organized zones, particles drift toward a few preferred angles around the axis, so strands appear — five in the semi-spiral, a spectrum of them at the King's Chamber, three in the building spiral. In the two turbulent zones that pull is gone and the strands scatter. That is the physics of mode selection and breakdown, made visible.

Compression calms. In the narrowing cone above Position 2 the turbulence dies away (relaminarization), a dark gap follows at the apex, and the flow bursts out as a jet. The return over the outside of the field is left undrawn here so the column stays clear; the particles you see spiraling in under the base are that return arriving.

Watching Zone by Zone

Zone 1 and the separatrix (red). Flow spirals in under the base from every side, speeding up and spinning faster as it nears the axis, and is captured by the core at the bell.

Zone 2 (red to orange). Streams from every side collide; the trails churn with no common direction, then a faint shared spin shows near the top.

Zone 3, the semi-spiral (yellow). The chaos finds its center: five threads gather, tighten and slow, then flare into the cradle.

Zone 4, the Queen's Chamber (green). The heart of the column. Inside, the Queen's Chamber with its sphere of light, and the heart's own toroidal field in deep emerald — rising up the outside, turning in over the top, falling down the axis, counter to the great field. Around it, the rising flow itself rides up over the onion-shaped membrane as a thick, translucent sheet: green-yellow at the root, true green at the equator, green-blue as it closes over the top. It slows at the wide equator and quickens toward the poles, as continuity requires. As Zone 3 gathers the chaos of Zone 2 into order at the base of the bubble, Zone 4 gathers its outer flow at the top — not into a point but into a collar about a quarter as wide as the equator, just above the upper stillpoint. The flow runs faster and fuller on the King's Chamber side, so it reaches that collar unevenly, and the unevenness decides how it leaves: the whole bundle swings toward the King's Chamber as it spins off, so the first release heads for the chamber, diffusing into the spectrum as it goes, before the column recentres and winds on upward. In the physics this is the wake above a breakdown bubble, where the core is pushed off the axis and begins to precess — the start of the spiral.

Zone 5, the King's Chamber (spectrum). At the King's Chamber the flow opens into the spectrum, red through violet, spiraling up the column. The spectrum runs three times around the column, so that a whole spectrum, every color from red to violet, passes through the chamber itself. At the chamber's level the lowest wave of the spectrum leans toward the chamber's side, swelling out through the room to touch its back wall, while the far side of the column keeps its shape (my reading, not Ra's). ~Ra describes the spiraling energy beginning to diffuse into red-through-violet color values at the King's Chamber position, with half of the diffusion angle passing through that chamber 56.6–56.7, and the light there configured in "the seven distinctive color, or energy, vibratory rates" 57.12.

Threshold 4 and Zone 6 (breakdown, then the red funnel). Above the King's Chamber the spectrum breaks down: the strands waver and grey, and the first helix lets go. Out of that loosened flow the second helix forms, a red spiral that narrows like the end of a funnel, winding and moving faster as it tightens (59.9).

Threshold 5, Position 2 (plum and grey). The building spiral comes apart.

Zone 7 (violet), the apex gap, and Zone 8 (gold). Calm, a pause, then emission.

What Ra Says

Ra calls the lowest spiral "the foundation semi-spiral which supplies the prana for all that may be affected by the three following upward spirals of light" 59.10. Of the Queen's Chamber position, Ra says the light "reaches the point of turning. This acts as a compression of the light" 59.13.

Of the higher position, Ra describes "a transformation across boundaries of dimension" that "causes an apparent diffusion and weakness of the spiraling energy," and adds that "in position two, as you have called it, much work may be done inter-dimensionally" 59.14. ~Ra places the third full spiral as radiating from the top of the pyramid 59.10.

The zone physics comes from the literature on vortex breakdown; the placement of Ra's spirals and positions on that physics is my reading.

Where the Model Bends

This is a kinematic model, not a full fluid simulation. I wrote down the speed, spin and radius of the flow zone by zone, following the physics of swirling columns, and let the particles move through that field. The turn counts and radii are set to match the July model so the two can be compared directly. A true Navier–Stokes solution of this geometry would differ in its details.

The turbulence is drawn as a jitter laid over the mean flow, the return circuit is schematic, and the time scale is chosen for watching, not measurement. The pyramid outline and chambers use the first model's coordinates (one world unit to about 31.5 cubits).

The green ray at the Queen's Chamber, the blue at the King's, and Position 2 at Threshold 5 are my synthesis; Ra doesn't set the energy centers along the pyramid's axis. I'm offering these thoughts for what they are worth.
