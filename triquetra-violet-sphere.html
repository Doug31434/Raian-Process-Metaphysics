About this model

What this shows

Three petals of one knot — red at the bottom, yellow at the upper left, blue at the upper right. It's a single strand, so following any petal outward and back brings me into the next one, and the strand crosses over and under itself at the center. The points of light traveling the strand take on each petal's color as they pass through it.

The whole knot sits inside a very faint violet sphere. For now the green sphere at the center is left out on purpose, so the weave itself can be seen. The strand is drawn as a flow of light more than a solid cord: a translucent sheath with pulses moving through it, and points of light spiraling along inside. An optional overlay (off for now) colors the places where red meets yellow orange and where red meets blue indigo.

What Ra says
"The red, yellow, and blue rays are primary because they signify activity of a primary nature." 41.25

~Ra describes red as the foundation, yellow as the ray of self-awareness and interaction, and blue as the first ray of radiation of self regardless of what another does 41.25. Ra calls violet constant, "the mark, the register, the identity, the true vibration of an entity" 39.10.

Ra gives orange "three petals" and indigo a form that is "triangular, or three-petaled" in many people 51.8.

What follows closely

If violet is the register of the whole, it makes sense to draw it as the field everything else sits inside, and not as one more station on a vertical line. And since orange and indigo are both three-petaled in Ra's description, the triquetra's own three-fold shape may already be echoing them.

My proposal

My proposal: the primaries are one strand, not three separate centers stacked on a ladder. Red, yellow and blue are the framework, and the knot shows them as a single movement folding through three kinds of activity. The rays between them arise where that movement meets itself: orange where the foundation touches self-awareness, indigo where the foundation touches the radiating self.

Open questions and limits

Where green belongs when it returns — at the crossing in the center, as before, or somewhere else. Whether orange and indigo belong at these contacts or need forms of their own. Why indigo arises from red and blue (in the usual order, indigo sits between blue and violet) — whether that's the point of the image or a slip in it. Whether red should stay at the bottom.

Color here is a teaching image. Arguments from how light or pigment mixes don't carry weight. I'm offering these thoughts for what they are worth.
